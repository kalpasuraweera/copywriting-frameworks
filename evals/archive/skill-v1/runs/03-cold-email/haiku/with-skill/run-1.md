# Cold Email: Copywriting Services to Seed-Stage Founder

## My Thinking (The Six P's)

**People:** Seed-stage founder, freshly funded, wearing 100 hats, skeptical of unsolicited pitches. Momentum is high but so is the pressure to hit traction metrics fast.

**Positioning:** Most post-raise founders focus on product and hiring. Messaging gets DIY'd or deprioritized. A copywriter who specializes in seed-stage companies and moves *fast* (3-4 weeks, not 3 months) offers a shortcut they don't see coming.

**Promise:** Clear positioning and converting copy *before* momentum cools — so the capital you raised actually translates to investor follow-ups and customer conversions.

**Proof:** Research on *their* specific company/situation (strongest proof in cold outreach — proves you're not spam). A concrete case study from another founder in their space is secondary.

**Priority:** The 60-90 day window post-raise is when positioning changes compound hardest on traction. After that, it's late.

**Process:** Pull positioning from customer conversations → build copy from real insight, not guesses.

---

## Hook Options

Score these against: benefit × relevance × credibility ÷ perceived effort

1. **Research-based (strongest):** "I looked at [Company], and the positioning angle that'd move your metrics most is [specific insight]. Right now, your copy points to [current gap]."
   - Shows work done + immediate value

2. **Moment-based:** "[Founder], you just closed seed. The founders who lock positioning *now* (while momentum is hot) get 6 months of compounding traction. The ones who wait? It takes 3x longer."
   - Urgency + specificity

3. **Insight-based (riskier):** "I noticed [Company] solves [Problem A], but what actually keeps your customers up at night is [Problem B]. Your copy talks about A. It should talk about B."
   - High credibility if you're right, risky if you're not

**Go with #1 for a cold email to a stranger** — it's hardest to ignore and doesn't assume too much.

## Full Email

```
Subject: [Founder name] — your positioning window

Hi [Founder name],

I looked at your announcement on [Crunchbase / TechCrunch / their blog], 
and I noticed something: you're positioning [Company] as [current positioning], 
but the angle that actually moves investor meetings and customer conversions 
is [specific insight about their market/product].

If you nail that positioning in the next 60 days, while capital is hot and 
you're recruiting, the messaging effect compounds all quarter.

Here's the pattern I see: most founders either DIY positioning (and it reads 
too insider-focused) or hire an agency (and wait 8 weeks while momentum cools). 
I do it differently — I work from your customer conversations to lock in positioning, 
then rewrite the copy that matters: homepage, pitch deck language, whatever sits 
in front of investors and early customers. Usually 3–4 weeks, start to finish.

[*Only include if you have this:*] I worked with a founder at [Company / similar space] 
who moved from 8 qualified leads per 100 visitors to 15 in the first month post-reposition 
— same traffic, just clearer messaging about what actually matters to the buyer.

Quick question: Is positioning still up for grabs on your end, or is it locked?

[Your name]
```

---

## What to Customize

- **[Founder name]** → their actual name (or just use it in the subject + drop it from the greeting)
- **[Company]** → their company name
- **[Crunchbase / TechCrunch / their blog]** → where you actually found the news
- **[current positioning]** → what their current copy/pitch says they do
- **[specific insight]** → a real observation about their market/product that their messaging hasn't landed yet (e.g., "you're positioning this as a dev tool, but what's actually driving adoption is ops teams who are tired of context-switching")
- **[Company / similar space]** → only include a case study if you have real numbers; otherwise, cut this paragraph entirely. Generic case studies weaken cold email.

---

## Why This Works

- **Opens with research** (you're not spam)
- **Leads with insight, not pitch** (you show value before asking for attention)
- **Names the moment** (they just raised — this is the *only* reason they act now)
- **One clear CTA** (a question, not a demand — keeps the door open)
- **Short enough to read in one sitting** (cold email = 30 seconds max)

If they reply at all, you've won. The goal is a reply, not a meeting.
