**Single biggest fix:** Replace the generic promise with a specific outcome for a specific person.

Right now you're saying "achieve more" and "unlock potential" — abstract words that apply to literally every competitor. The reader doesn't know what changes about *their* actual day.

**Supporting diagnosis:** You don't have a specific avatar. "Businesses of all sizes" isn't a person — it's the opposite. The moment you pick a real audience, the copy sharpens. For example:

- If it's "overloaded remote teams toggling between 7 apps," the promise becomes: "keep everyone on one page instead of scattered across Slack, email, Google Drive, and a spreadsheet."
- If it's "fast-growing startups losing context as you hire," the promise becomes: "onboard new team members in 30 minutes, not three weeks of digging through scattered docs."

The features you list (task management, chat, file sharing) are fine — but they only matter once the reader sees themselves in the problem you're solving.

**Secondary issue:** "Trusted by businesses of all sizes" proves nothing. Once you have a real customer story (even just a name and one sentence about what changed), it lands 10x harder.

Start by answering: who's your ideal customer right now, and what's the one thing they waste the most time on?
