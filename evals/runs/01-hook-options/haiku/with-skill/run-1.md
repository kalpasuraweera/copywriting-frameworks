# 5 Hook Options

1. **"You know that guilt? Ordering takeout for the third time this week because you 'don't have time to cook'? We figured out how to stop it."**

2. **"Meal kits usually feel like fancy assembly. These actually teach you *how* to cook — in 30 minutes, with your kids watching."**

3. **"We were spending $1200/year on takeout. One change gave us home-cooked dinners, real cooking skills, and $400 back. Here's how."**

4. **"The difference between 'I can't cook on a weeknight' and 'I can cook dinner in 30 min' is just one system. Here's what we changed."**

5. **"Real talk: teaching your kids to cook matters. But 'let's make a 3-hour meal from scratch' doesn't work when you're exhausted. This does."**

---

**Notes:**

- **Hook #1** leans hardest on guilt/relatability — lands best if the brand's real story involves overcoming that exact tension.
- **Hook #3** works if you have real data on cost savings; if not, swap the number for a concrete example ("went from $40 takeout nights to $12 home dinners").
- **All assume** the product actually delivers speed + simplicity + something "real" (actual cooking, not just plating). Adjust to match your actual positioning.
