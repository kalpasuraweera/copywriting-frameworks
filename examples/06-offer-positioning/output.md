At $3k a month you're priced well above a generalist bookkeeper. So the copy can't just say "we do bookkeeping." It has to show why getting e-commerce books wrong costs far more than $3k. Here's how I'd set that up.

## Your Six P's

**People**
Don't target "e-commerce brands." Target this person: **the founder or operator of a Shopify brand (maybe also on Amazon) doing $1–5M a year and selling physical products.** They have somewhere between 2 and 15 people on the team. They still check the bank balance before placing an inventory order. Their books are done by a generalist, or by themselves in QuickBooks, and they usually run a month or two behind.
- *What they fear:* a surprise tax bill, or running out of cash right before Q4 inventory is due.
- *What frustrates them:* Shopify says they made money, and the bank account disagrees.
- *What they want:* to know their real profit per product, so they can scale ads and reorder stock with confidence.

If you mostly serve one channel mix (say, Shopify-only DTC brands), narrow it to that. It'll make every line sharper.

**Positioning**
Generalist bookkeepers are cheaper, but they don't understand e-commerce money flows. The strongest positioning is a true, specific fact your competitors never mention, something like:
> "We match every Shopify, Amazon, and PayPal payout to the individual orders, fees, and refunds inside it, every month."

*Only use this if you actually do it.* Whatever your real version is, that's the line.

**Promise** (running the "so what?" test)
- We close your books monthly. → *So what?*
- You get an accurate P&L, including cost of goods sold. → *So what?*
- You know your real margin on every product. → *So what?*
- You reorder inventory and set ad budgets based on real numbers, not your bank balance. → *So what?*
- **You stop getting surprised, and you run the company like someone who knows their numbers.**

Promise, in one line: **"Know your real profit by the [10th] of every month."**

**Proof**
- *Tier 1 (who you're associated with):* `[Shopify/Amazon/Xero/QBO partner status, certifications]`
- *Tier 2 (your results):* `[number of e-commerce brands served, a total you've found in mis-booked costs, if you've tracked it]`
- *Tier 3 (strongest, a client like them):* `[a founder of a $1–5M brand describing the margin surprise you uncovered]`. Go get this one first. A founder in the same revenue range will trust a peer's story more than any number.

**Priority**
- *Cost of doing nothing:* if cost of goods is booked wrong, the margin looks better than it is. That means buying too much inventory and scaling ads on products that are losing money. It gets more expensive the bigger the brand grows.
- *Real deadlines:* quarter-end, tax filing dates, and the Q4 inventory buy.
- *Scarcity:* only mention limited spots if you really do cap clients. `[e.g. "we onboard N brands per month"]`

**Process**
> "Most people think bookkeeping is data entry. For e-commerce it isn't. The hard part is matching payouts, fees, refunds, and inventory to the actual orders, and generalists skip that step. That's why your P&L doesn't match your bank. We start there."

**Taking the risk off the buyer (optional, only if you can deliver it)**
> "If your books aren't closed by the [10th], that month is free."

This turns $3k from a leap of faith into a promise you're accountable for. That's the easiest way to justify the price.

## Sample ad (Meta / LinkedIn)

**Hook options**

| Hook | Benefit | Relevance | Credibility | Effort elsewhere |
|------|---------|-----------|-------------|------------------|
| **"Shopify says you made money last month. Your bank account disagrees."** | High | Very high (almost every brand has felt this) | High (it's their own experience) | High |
| "Bookkeeping for 7- and 8-figure e-commerce brands." | Low | Medium | Medium | Low |
| "Your margins are probably wrong." | Medium | High | Low (feels like a cheap shot) | Medium |

Going with the first. It opens with a feeling the reader already has, not a description of your service.

**Ad copy**

> **Shopify says you made money last month. Your bank account disagrees.**
>
> The gap is usually hiding in your books. Payouts never got matched to orders. Fees and refunds got lumped together. Inventory got recorded as a cost when you paid the supplier, not when the product sold.
>
> A generalist bookkeeper won't catch that, because they don't do e-commerce. We only work with brands doing $1–5M a year. Every month we match each payout down to the order and close your books by the [10th]. You see real profit per product *before* you reorder stock or raise ad spend.
>
> `["Quote from a client founder about what you found." — Name, Brand]`
>
> **Doing $1–5M on Shopify or Amazon? Book a free 20-minute books review this month. We'll show you exactly where your P&L and your bank balance don't match. [Book your review →]**

**CTA check:** *Who:* $1–5M brands on Shopify or Amazon. *What:* book a review. *When:* this month. *How:* the button. The review also shows your expertise before they pay you anything.
