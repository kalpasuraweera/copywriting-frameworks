### Why it isn't converting

> "Interested in learning more? Feel free to reach out anytime."

A CTA needs four things: **who** it's for, **what** to do, **when**, and **how**. This one is missing all four.

- **Who:** nobody. It's written for anyone who happens to be "interested."
- **What:** "learn more" and "reach out" aren't actions. Reach out how? To learn what?
- **When:** "anytime" means *no reason to do it today*, so in practice, never.
- **How:** no link, no button, no reply keyword. The reader has to work out the next step on their own.

"Feel free" also sounds apologetic, as if you're sorry to be asking. A CTA should be a clear, confident invitation.

### The rewrite

I don't know your offer or audience yet, so here's the pattern first, then three versions for the most common situations. Pick the one that fits and swap in your real details.

**Pattern:** `[Who it's for] → [the one action] → [when / what happens next] → [exact mechanism]`

**If you sell a service (calls, consulting, agency work):**
> Running a [specific type of business]? Book a free 20-minute call this week and I'll show you the [one specific problem] costing you the most. **[Pick a time →]**

**If you sell software or a product:**
> Managing [specific job] for a team of [size]? Start your free [14]-day trial today. Setup takes about [5] minutes. **[Start free trial]**

**If this is the end of a post, email, or newsletter:**
> Want the [specific resource] I use for this? Reply "[keyword]" and I'll send it over today.

### One thing to avoid

Don't fix "anytime" by inventing a fake deadline ("only 3 spots left!") unless it's true. When there's no real deadline, make the *when* about what happens next: "this week," "today," "I'll reply within a day." That still gives people a reason to act now without costing you trust.
