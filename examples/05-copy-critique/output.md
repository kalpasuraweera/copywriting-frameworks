## The single biggest thing to fix: it isn't written for anyone

"Teams." "Your organization." "Businesses of all sizes." The paragraph never says who it's for, and that's the root problem. Every other weakness comes from it:

- You can't state an outcome without knowing whose problem you're solving, **so** the promise turns into a feature list.
- You can't choose proof that feels relatable without knowing who has to relate to it, **so** you get "trusted by businesses of all sizes."
- You can't write like a real person talking, **so** it falls back on "empower," "seamlessly," and "unlock its full potential."

Choose one specific reader and most of the other fixes become obvious.

---

## Full diagnosis

**Six P's**

| P | What's there | Problem |
|---|--------------|---------|
| People | "teams," "businesses of all sizes" | Nobody in particular. A 4-person agency and a 400-person enterprise have different problems. |
| Positioning | "all-in-one platform" | Every competitor says this, so it doesn't set you apart. |
| Promise | "streamline workflows, boost productivity, unlock full potential" | These are abstractions. None of them survives a "so what?" Nobody can picture "unlocked potential." |
| Proof | "Trusted by businesses of all sizes" | Can't be checked and doesn't name anyone. Reads as filler. |
| Priority | "Get started today!" | An exclamation mark isn't a reason. There's no cost of waiting. |
| Process | a list of four features | Doesn't say why current tools fail or why this works differently. |

**Five C's**

- **Clear:** fails the firefighter test. Try shouting "robust suite of cutting-edge features" across a burning room.
- **Concise:** "all-in-one," "robust suite," "cutting-edge," "advanced," "seamlessly." That's five modifiers doing no work.
- **Concrete:** there's nothing to picture. No moment, no person, no number.
- **Conversational:** no one talks like this. It sounds like a brochure.
- **Cadence:** three sentences that all run the same length and shape, ending in a cliché CTA. It reads flat.

## Rewrite (to show the direction)

To make this concrete I had to pick a reader, so I chose **owners of 5–20 person agencies juggling client projects.** If your real buyer is someone else, the structure stays the same and the details change.

> **Know what's late before your client does.**
>
> If you run a small agency, client work is probably spread across Slack threads, email chains, and a spreadsheet that only one person updates. So you find out a deadline slipped when the client emails to ask.
>
> Teamory puts every project, file, and conversation for a client in one place. A two-minute look on Monday morning tells you what's on track and what isn't.
>
> `["Real quote from an agency owner about what changed." — Name, Agency]`
>
> **Running a 5–20 person agency? Start a free trial and set up your first client in about [10] minutes. [Start free trial]**

**What changed:**
- **Who it's for:** "teams" became agency owners with 5–20 people.
- **Promise:** a feature list became an outcome they can picture (no more hearing about slipped deadlines from the client).
- **Proof:** a vague claim became a slot for a real, relatable testimonial. It's left as a placeholder because none was provided.
- **Cadence:** the short headline and short final line frame the longer sentences in between.
- **CTA:** it now names who it's for, what to do, how long setup takes, and gives one button.
